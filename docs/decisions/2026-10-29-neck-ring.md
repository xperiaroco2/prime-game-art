# The standard neck ring for bodies A and B (art #196)

Date: 2026-10-29. Status: accepted (the engineer's pick in D1: the head as its own piece with one standard neck ring
for both bodies, already on the drawing). Stands beside the body-types ADR on branch `feat/alien-body-b-196`
(`2026-10-29-body-types.md`, not on `main` yet); when that lands, this one is its addendum.

## Context
The heads are welded on the body's open neck loop today (`alien_char_build.py`, `docs/contract.md`). B1 built body B
on A's topology and kept A's loop, its x and y, dropping it 6.5 cm. Step D2 draws both bodies in mm from their
approved concepts (`tools/blender/body_dims/A.toml`, `B.toml`, `body_dims_measure.py`) and needs one ring every head
and both bodies share, so that any head goes on any body. The research (R1, point 2) asks for the same loop's vertex
count, order and positions on every head and body, weights only on Neck and Head, normals copied from the body.

## Decision
- **The ring is body A's open neck loop as it is** (S4b `alien_body.blend`, measured by `body_dims_probe.py`):
  24 vertices in the mesh's order, flat (tilt 0 degrees), centre x 0, y +47.3 mm (behind the midline), half-widths
  56.7 mm across and 52.8 mm front to back (an outline 113.4 x 105.6 mm). Every head made so far fits it, so no head
  changes. A's concept neck at the ring is a little fuller (132 x 116 mm): the body's neck blends into the ring over
  the 30 mm under it, the ring itself does not grow. B's concept draws the line lower, at the collar, where the
  shoulders' slopes already start: her outline at the ring is 229 mm across (at least 125 mm deep from the side within 60 mm under it), and her
  narrowest neck (121 mm across) is 79 mm above the ring, in the head's piece; her body meets the ring with its
  slopes, the 30 mm blend under it the same.
- **Where the ring is read:** the concept's drawn ring line, a thin dark line across most of the neck, searched from
  the chin down to the shoulder line (`body_dims_geom.ring_line`); not the chin's broad shadow, and not only inside
  the neck's narrowest band (B's neck widens before the line). A row 282 (1.9386 mm per px), B row 292 (1.9226; the
  line runs over rows 291 to 294, sagging in the middle, and the ring is the median of the columns' darkest rows).
- **Height:** A 1390 mm; B 1324.7 mm (0.953 of A, f's ratio, as B1). Only z differs; x, y, count and order are equal.
- **The bones around it:** the Head bone's head 48.3 mm above the ring and the Neck bone's head 50.8 mm under it, on
  both bodies (A's offsets): A Neck 1339.2 and Head 1438.3, B Neck 1273.9 and Head 1373.0 (the tables' `[skeleton]`
  takes both from the ring, not from A's heights times 0.953). B1 scaled B's Neck head (48.5 mm under); B's build in
  D3 sets it to 50.8 mm so the Neck-to-Head offset is A's, which a head skinned to Neck and Head needs to follow any body's Head by name.
- **The split:** the head's mesh ends at the ring from above (its own bottom loop equals the ring), the body's mesh
  ends at it from below (its top open loop). The ring's vertices carry weights only on Neck and Head (the head's
  bottom loop and the body's top loop the same weights), and their normals are copied from the body and exported as
  custom normals, so the seam shades as one surface.
- The tables hold it under `[neck_ring]` (`z`, `vertices`, `centre_y`, `half_x`, `half_y`, `tilt_deg`,
  `head_bone_above`, `neck_bone_below`, and the concept's own neck there as `concept_w`, `concept_d`);
  `tools/tests/test_body_dims_geom.py` holds both tables to one ring.

## Consequences
- The head is a separate piece in D3 and later: its bottom loop must be the ring's 24 vertices exactly; a head made to
  another loop is refitted to this one, never the ring to the head.
- A 32-vertex ring (rounder at close range) would need every head refitted; not worth it while the seam hides under
  collars at game distance. Revisit with the face close-ups if the ring shows facets.
