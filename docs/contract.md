# The character contract

The contract says what every character model, clothing piece, accessory and prop must be so that one skeleton, one
animation set and one material serve them all, whichever generator or artist made them. It is data, not prose:

| File | What it holds | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| `contract/humanoid.json` | Godot's `SkeletonProfileHumanoid`: every bone's name, parent, reference pose, group, tail and handle data | generated by `contract` from the game's pinned Godot; never edited by hand |
| `contract/contract.toml` | axes, height, skeleton rules, sockets, slots, animation rules, budgets, and what `check` enforces per kind | by hand, in a PR |
| `contract/bone_maps/*.toml` | renames from a vendor rig (Mixamo, Meshy, Quaternius) to the profile names | by hand; unconfirmed maps say so |

The source is the ADR sketch in the
[wave 1 research report](https://github.com/xperiaroco2/prime-game/issues/165#issuecomment-5953165083) on
xperiaroco2/prime-game#165, with
[the engineer's answers](https://github.com/xperiaroco2/prime-game/issues/165#issuecomment-5954455064) of 2026-10-02:
five fingers for gestures, clothing in pieces with sets as presets, eyes and mouth changeable like the hair, one
neutral body in v1, animations only (no ragdoll). Since 2026-10-11 the knockdown is a ragdoll ("Ragdoll" below).

## Commands

```
tools/run.py contract              # regenerate contract/humanoid.json from Godot, validate contract/
tools/run.py contract --check      # change nothing; fail when humanoid.json is stale or contract/ disagrees
tools/run.py check <model> --kind body|clothing|accessory|prop [--map mixamo|meshy|quaternius] [--report FILE]
tools/run.py check <model> --slot hair_or_hat|eyes|mouth|top|... [--map NAME] [--report FILE]
tools/run.py rename-bones <model> --map <name> --out <file.glb|.gltf|.blend>
```

`contract` runs `tools/godot/dump_profile.gd` in the Godot named by `GODOT_BIN` (headless, it quits by itself) and
refuses any version but the pinned one. The JSON has sorted keys, bones in profile order and floats rounded to six
places, so the same Godot always writes the same file. Every Godot API name in the script was checked against the
game repo's `tools/out/godot-api/4.7.2/extension_api.json`. `--check` also validates `contract.toml` and the bone maps
against the profile: every bone they name exists, every socket sits on a profile bone, every profile bone is either
weighted, optional or a finger, no two map entries rename to the same bone.

`check` measures the model in headless Blender (`tools/blender/check_model.py`) and judges the numbers in plain Python
(`tools/runner/commands/_checks.py`), so every rule has a test without Blender. It writes `report.json` (default
`tools/out/check/<model>/report.json`) with the verdict, one result per check (`pass`, `warn` or `fail`) and the raw
measurements, prints the same, and exits 1 when any check fails. Loads `.glb`, `.gltf`, `.fbx`, `.obj` and `.blend`.
`--slot` judges a piece for one slot of `contract.toml`: the slot's `check_kind` and its own `budget`, so hair
is held to `head_item` rather than the accessory budget. `--kind` alone uses the kind's budget. `contract --check`
also rejects a `kinds.<kind>` rule outside its values (`fail`, `warn`, `off`; `required` or `forbidden`; `all` or
`subset`).

`rename-bones` strips the map's prefix, renames the bones (Blender renames the vertex groups with them, so the skin
follows), deletes the bones the map lists under `drop` and saves to `--out`. The source file is never changed. A
dropped bone's children move to its parent without moving (they are disconnected first), and its weights move to its
nearest ancestor that stays: an end bone past the last finger joint never moves apart from that joint, so the skin
deforms the same. Only a weighted dropped bone without such an ancestor is kept and reported. `check --map` reads the
rig the same way, so a file that passes `check --map` passes `check` after `rename-bones`. Run `check` on the result.

## What the contract says, and why

**Skeleton.** Exactly the 56 bones of Godot's humanoid profile, with its names and parents, so Godot's retargeting
and one shared `AnimationLibrary` work on every body without per-model bone maps. The list comes from Godot itself:
typing it would drift. `Root` sits on the ground between the feet and carries no weights. 22 bones must carry weights
(the trunk, and per side the shoulder, both arm bones, the hand, both leg bones, the foot and the toes); `Jaw` and the
eyes may. Five fingers per hand are present and weighted, because gestures need them (three bones each; the thumb's
first is the metacarpal). No extra bones: sockets, jiggle and helper bones would be dropped by Godot's `BoneMap` anyway,
and sockets live in the Godot scene instead.

**Axes and size.** glTF, Y up, 1 unit = 1 m, front +Z (`Vector3.MODEL_FRONT`; Blender shows it as -Y, and the
character's left at +X). Feet at y = 0 and the origin between them, transforms applied, so a model drops into the
scene without fix-up nodes. The character scene turns the model 180 degrees to face -Z, like the greybox. Height
1.75 m (1.70 to 1.80 allowed, the ADR sketch's window) with the eyes near 1.6 m: the game's capsule is 1.8 m tall with
a 0.4 m radius and the camera sits at 1.6 m, so a taller body would poke out of its own collision shape and the
first-person view would not match the body. **The plasticine look** (the baked clay characters, `godot-check
--textured`) may stand 1.70 to 1.90 m (`plasticine_height_max_m`), the eyes window 1.6 +- 0.08 m unchanged: its head
is 1.3 times the pack's, with hair on top, so a character scaled until its eyes are at the camera's 1.6 m stands
about 1.85 m. The engineer chose that scale (`eyes16`, 2026-10-12, art PR #139) over a shorter body with the eyes
below the camera. The hair top then passes the 1.8 m capsule by a few cm; the game side decides if that matters. T-pose rest, arms horizontal and palms down, the pose Godot's profile uses.

**Weights.** At most 4 influences per vertex (one glTF joint set, and the budget's assumption for about 20
skinned bodies on screen), none below 0.01, summing to 1.

**Sockets.** `Socket_Head` (Head), `Socket_Face` (Head), `Socket_HandR` (RightHand), `Socket_HandL` (LeftHand),
`Socket_Carry` (Chest), `Socket_Belt` (Hips, left hip), `Socket_Back` (UpperChest). Each is a `Marker3D` under a
`BoneAttachment3D` in the character scene, and every item or accessory has a `Grip` marker that lands on it. The
hand, carry and belt start from the greybox's points in `client/player/remote_player_body.gd` (hand
`(0.45, 0.95, -0.15)`, belt `(-0.48, 0.85, 0)`, carry `(0, 0.8, -0.72)`, all facing -Z); `contract.toml` stores each
also in model space (front +Z, the same point turned 180 degrees). The others are measured on the first base body.

**Slots (v1).** `skin_colour` (a palette index), `eyes` and `mouth` (face slots: a set of morphs on the template head,
"Faces" below, that provides the slot's listed expressions as morphs over it, so blinking and talking need no mesh
change; plus the slot's truly separate parts, eyeballs or teeth and tusks, as rigid pieces on `Socket_Face`, whose
only morphs are the eyeballs' look morphs, `part_morphs`), `hair_or_hat`,
`face_accessory` and `back_item` (rigid pieces on their sockets), `top`, `bottom` and `shoes` (skinned to profile
bones). A set is a preset of pieces, one per slot. Cosmetics are public data and never derived from a role.

**Faces.** Faces are morphs (blend shapes, glTF morph targets) on one template head (the engineer, 2026-10-18; art
#200, `docs/decisions/2026-10-18-faces-as-morphs.md`). The mouth, lips and lids are deformations of the head's own
surface, not pieces laid on top and not frames in a face texture. Separate meshes only for what truly is separate:
eyeballs, teeth, tusks, hair, crests, hats and clothes (`[face].separate`). Every head shape is a wrap of the template,
the same vertices in the same order, so each eye and mouth morph works on every head: N heads, M mouths and K eyes are
N + M + K pieces, not N x M x K. A face morph moves only skin whose heaviest bone weight is on `Head`, `Jaw`,
`LeftEye` or `RightEye` (`[face].morph_bones`; a vertex tied between one of them and another bone, such as 0.5 `Head`
and 0.5 `Neck`, counts as on the head; weights within two 8-bit steps, 2/255, are a tie, as glTF may store them). One tolerance: a soft jaw or chin morph's falloff may move skin on `Neck`
(`[face].edge_bones`) by at most 2 mm (`edge_mm`, provisional), a shift a collar fitted to the neck hides. The key must
also move head skin (neck skin alone is a body shape), and every bone a vertex is tied to must be an edge bone (a
`Chest`-`Neck` tie is body skin); past that, or on any other bone, it is a body-shape morph, and there are none (`blend_shapes_body = 0`), because every skinned
outfit would need the same keys or the body would poke through it. A shape key that moves nothing counts as a face
morph (it costs only file size). More than `face_morphs_target` (64, provisional) face morphs on one mesh warn. The
eyes slot's eyeballs may carry their look morphs (`look_l`, `look_r`, `look_u`, `look_d`, the slot's `part_morphs`):
they pass under `check --slot eyes`; any other shape key on a separate part warns.

**The alien (exception).** The alien's face is overlaid rigid parts, not morphs: eyeballs with rotating lid shells and
replacement mouth pieces on the Head bone's sockets, never mesh deformation (the engineer, 2026-10-19; art #221,
`docs/decisions/2026-10-19-alien-overlaid-face-four-fingers.md`, which supersedes "Faces" above for the alien). It has
four fingers (Index, Middle, Ring, Thumb); the Little finger's bones stay in the skeleton, unweighted, so the skeleton
and the clips are unchanged. The five weighted fingers in "Skeleton" above and `check --kind body`'s finger rule still
describe the human bodies; the alien's own rule is a follow-up.

**Animation.** Skeletal clips on the contract skeleton in one shared library, in place, loops suffixed `_loop`; the
knockdown is a ragdoll ("Ragdoll" below); every other state is a clip. Eyes and mouth animate by the face morphs
("Faces" above), weighted at runtime, never by bones or texture frames; the full expression lists wait for the style
answers (H2).

**Ragdoll.** The knockdown is a ragdoll (the engineer, 2026-10-11, prime-game#516 answer 3; art #115): the body
falls under physics at once, a dead body keeps its ragdoll pose, a revived player stands up at once; the knockdown,
lying-downed and get-up clips are gone. Idle, walk, run, carry, pick up and put down and crouch stay clips.
- *Who builds it.* The art side measures the ragdoll data per body type from the character's GLB
  (`tools/run.py ragdoll`) and ships it in the character package as `export/<id>.ragdoll.json` (listed in
  `package.json`). The game builds the PhysicalBone3D nodes from it at import under a PhysicalBoneSimulator3D;
  `godot/ragdoll/builder.gd` is the reference builder the game copies (`build`, `start`, `stop`).
- *The data.* 16 bodies: `Body` (the pelvis, the root body; `Hips` follows it), `Abdomen`, `Torso` (the chest),
  `Neck` (the neck and head as one body, shaped to the head and hair), and per side `UpperArm`, `LowerArm`, `Wrist`,
  `UpperLeg`, `LowerLeg`, `Foot`. Fingers, toes, `Hips`, `Chest`, the shoulders, the head and the sockets follow their
  simulated owner (`followers`). Per body: its bone (the export's 64-bone names) and contract `profile` name, the
  shape (`box` with `size`, or `capsule` along the bone's Y with `radius` and `height`), `offset` and `rotation`
  (a quaternion) in the bone's rest frame, `mass_share` and `mass_kg` (70 kg in all), and its joint to its physical
  parent: `cone` (`swing_deg`, `twist_deg`), `hinge` (`axis` in the bone frame, `min_deg`..`max_deg`, positive the
  anatomical bend) or `pin`, with its `anchor` in the bone frame. `material` holds friction, bounce and damping;
  `collision` the layer rule. Units are metres, kilograms and degrees; limits are from the standing pose.
- *Collision.* Every body is on the `downed` layer (3) with the `world` mask (1) only: a ragdoll never collides with
  players or other ragdolls (prime-game#754).
- *Sockets.* No socket changes: the BoneAttachment3D sockets (the carried package, hair and hats) follow their bones,
  which follow the simulated bodies; the fall proof asserts it.
- *Proof.* `tools/run.py ragdoll <glb> --fall` (Godot 4.7.2, Jolt Physics, headless) asserts the fall; `--shots DIR`
  shoots it off-screen (docs/godot.md, "The ragdoll").

**Budgets.** Above the target warns, above the cap fails. The character budgets are the engineer's decision of
2026-10-18 (quality over low-end hardware: the target is a mid-range gaming PC, RTX 3060/4060 class), measured with
`tools/run.py bench` (docs/godot.md, "Bench") and recorded in `docs/decisions/2026-10-18-character-budgets.md` (art
#212). They replace the foundation ADR's unmeasured sketch (character 8,000 / 12,000, textures 1,024, GLB 5 MB), which
forced the alien base from its 200k sculpt down to 11.7k triangles.

| Budget | Triangles target | Triangles cap | Vertices target / cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assembled character, LOD0 (not checked yet) | 40,000 | 60,000 | 30,000 / 45,000 |
| Base body (five fingers, head included) | 22,000 | 30,000 | 16,000 / 24,000 |
| Template head (part of the body; the only mesh with face morphs; provisional) | 12,000 | 15,000 | 7,000 / 9,000 |
| Clothing piece (provisional) | 3,000 | 5,000 | |
| Hair or hat (provisional) | 3,000 | 4,500 | |
| Face accessory, back or belt item; the eyes' and mouth's parts (provisional) | 1,500 | 2,500 | |
| Prop or third-person hand item | 1,500 | 2,000 | |

The pieces add up: the body plus one piece per slot at their targets (22,000 + 3 x 3,000 + 3,000 + 4 x 1,500) is the
character's 40,000, and at their caps 59,500, inside its 60,000. Body skin an outfit covers is cut out at assembly, so
a dressed character stays near the target. `check` judges one file against its kind's budget and does not judge the
assembled character yet. The piece budgets are starting points (the old ones times 2 to 4) to revise on the first real
pieces. Triangles count after triangulation; vertices count as imported, split at UV seams and hard edges, close to
what the GPU draws (a glTF file always comes split).

**Limits.** Textures: 2,048 px for the character's main set (base colour, normal and at most one ORM or roughness map,
`texture_maps_max`), VRAM Compressed in the game; 1,024 for LOD1 and LOD2 and for accessories and small parts.
Surfaces 6 target, 8 cap; 4 bone influences per vertex. Face morphs: 52 to 64 cover a full face rig (`face_morphs_target`
64), only on the separate head mesh: Godot keeps every morph dense, about 20 bytes per head vertex per morph in video
memory, once per unique head mesh (not per copy), so morph memory follows the head's vertex count. GLB cap 40 MB for a
character (LOD0, hand-made LODs, 64 head morphs and 2,048 textures with headroom; the bench's 40k stand-in with 64
morphs on an 8,900-vertex head is 29.6 MB, about 39 bytes per head vertex per morph), 4 MB for an accessory.

**Levels of detail** (`[lods]`). LOD0 40,000 / 60,000; LOD1 15,000 / 20,000 from about 4 m; LOD2 6,000 / 8,000 from
about 10 m; an optional LOD3 2,000 / 3,000 for far bodies and corpses from about 25 m; distances at 1440p and a 75
degree FOV, to be tuned in playtest. Godot's automatic import LODs stay on: they are index buffers over LOD0's vertices
(skinning and morphs still run over every LOD0 vertex), and in the bench they halved the characters' GPU time (20
bodies 1.44 ms at LOD0 only, 0.64 ms with them). Hand-made LOD meshes under visibility ranges and a low-poly shadow
caster (`cast_shadow = SHADOWS_ONLY`, the drawn meshes' shadows off) are optional, for when a profile shows skinning,
shadow or memory cost: on top of the automatic LODs the bench measured no saving from either at 20 bodies, in its one
scene (a small meeting ring: only 5 of the 20 bodies beyond the hand-made LOD2's 10 m; a spread-out crowd was not
measured).

## What `check` checks

| Check | body | clothing | accessory | prop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| triangles and vertices against the kind's budget | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| n-gons, loose vertices and edges, non-manifold edges (more than two faces on an edge): fail | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| open edges, debris parts under 4 faces, UVs outside 0..1, unapplied modifiers, extra materials: warn | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| a UV map on every mesh; location 0, rotation 0, scale 1 on every mesh and armature | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| height 1.70 to 1.80 m (plasticine look 1.90 m); feet at y = 0 | fail | | | feet warn |
| eyes near 1.6 m (from the eye bones) | warn | | | |
| facing: toes towards +Z, left hand at +X | fail | | | |
| one armature, skinned; no extra bones; parents as in the profile; duplicate names after the map | yes | yes | | |
| every profile bone present; the 22 weighted bones carry weights; five weighted finger chains | yes | | | |
| at most 4 weights per vertex, no unweighted vertex, nothing on Root | yes | yes | | |
| no armature and no skin (a rigid piece) | | | yes | yes |
| shape keys that move skin off the head bones (body-shape morphs; `Neck` skin up to 2 mm excepted): fail; face morphs on the head bones pass, more than 64 on one mesh warn | yes | | | |
| any shape key: warn (under `check --slot eyes`, the eyeballs' look morphs pass) | | yes | yes | yes |

Height is measured from the lowest to the highest vertex in the rest pose. Topology checks weld vertices closer
than 0.01 mm first, because glTF stores a vertex once per UV seam and hard edge. The glTF importer's bone display
shape is ignored. For each shape key `check` records the vertices it moves (more than 0.01 mm from its relative key)
grouped by the bone with each vertex's largest weight, with the count and the largest move in mm (`shape_key_bones`
in the report's measure: `{key: {bone: {"vertices", "max_mm"}}}`). Only vertex groups that are armature bones count
(a clay `.blend`'s `clay_border` group does not); bones tied for the largest weight (within 0.0001) are joined by `|`
(`Head|Neck`); the bones are named through the bone map; a vertex on no bone (`""`) counts as off the head.

## Bone maps

`mixamo.toml` follows Mixamo's published naming (`mixamorig:Hips`, `Spine1` for Chest, `LeftHandPinky3` for
LeftLittleDistal); Mixamo rigs have no `Root` and no `Jaw`, so `check --map mixamo` reports them missing until they
are added in Blender. Its `confirmed = true` means "from the published naming": no Mixamo file has been checked yet.

`quaternius.toml` is confirmed from the real rig
([#5](https://github.com/xperiaroco2/prime-game-art/issues/5)): the Universal Animation Library
(`UAL1_Standard.glb`) and the Universal Base Characters (`Superhero_Male_FullBody.gltf`) share one rig of 65 bones
with Unreal-mannequin names (`pelvis`, `spine_01`, `clavicle_l`), except `Head`. It has no twist or IK bones. Its 12
end bones (`thumb_04_leaf_l` ... `pinky_04_leaf_r`, `ball_leaf_l`, `ball_leaf_r`) are dropped; the Base Characters
weight a few vertices to the finger ones, and those weights move to the distal joint. It has no `Jaw` and no eye
bones, so `check --map quaternius` reports those three missing until they are added in Blender. The test
`test_the_quaternius_map_fits_the_real_rig` pins the 65 names and parents as measured (no raw file is committed).

`meshy.toml` is confirmed from Meshy's auto-rig of 2026-10-18 (batch `2026-10-18-alien-3d-base2`, `t1-rig` and
`t2-rig`, [#196](https://github.com/xperiaroco2/prime-game-art/issues/196)): 24 bones with Mixamo-style limb names and
no prefix, and a spine that counts down from the hips, `Hips` > `Spine02` > `Spine01` > `Spine` > `neck` (lower case)
> `Head`, with both shoulders under `Spine`; so `Spine02` is the profile's `Spine` and `Spine` its `UpperChest`. The
two helper ends `head_end` and `headfront` are dropped. The rig has no finger, eye, jaw or root bones (each hand is one
bone), so a Meshy-rigged body misses those until they are added in Blender. The map keeps the Mixamo-style finger
names for a later rig that has them. The test `test_the_meshy_map_fits_the_real_rig` pins the 24 names and parents as
measured (no raw file is committed).

## Tests

`tools/tests/test_contract_data.py` and `test_contract_checks.py` need nothing but Python.
`test_contract_tools.py` runs `contract --check` in Godot and builds fixtures in Blender with
`tools/blender/make_contract_fixture.py`: a 1.75 m box-mesh body on every profile bone (one box per bone, fully
weighted to it), a copy with defects (extra and missing bones, five weights, an n-gon, a non-manifold fin, too tall,
facing backwards), a copy with Mixamo names and end bones and without Root and Jaw, as Mixamo rigs come, a copy with Quaternius names
and end bones (one of them weighted) for the maps and
`rename-bones`, and a copy with connected chains that drops a middle bone. Without Godot or Blender
those tests skip with a message naming the variable to set.
